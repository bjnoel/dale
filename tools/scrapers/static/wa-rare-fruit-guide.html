What Grows Well in Perth and Southwest WA

Perth's Mediterranean climate (hot dry summers, mild wet winters) suits more fruit than most people expect. These are all well known among WA collectors, and you can buy them from local nurseries.

Avocado
Hass and Shepard dominate. Reed does well in hot spots. Need good drainage.
Fig
One of the easiest fruit trees to grow here. Brown Turkey, White Adriatic and lots of heritage varieties do really well.
Mango
Honey Gold, Kensington Pride, Palmer. Perth is marginal for mango, so give it a north-facing wall in a frost-free spot.
Lychee
Seedless varieties are getting easier to find. Likes it warm and humid, so it's hard work in Perth but possible in the coastal northern suburbs.
Pomegranate
Very drought-hardy. Wonderful, Parfianka, Salavatski. One of the easiest rare fruit to look after in Perth.
Mulberry
Shahtoot (white mulberry) is the most popular. Fruits October-December. Grows fast.
Feijoa
Underrated in WA. Also called pineapple guava. Fruits April-June. Grafted varieties (Mammoth, Unique) do better than seedlings.
Jujube
Chinese red date. Handles heat and drought really well. Fruits March-April. The rarer varieties are hard to find. Rare
Finger Lime
Australian native. Get a grafted tree, because seedlings take 5+ years. Popular and often out of stock.
Jaboticaba
Slow, but worth it. Fruits straight off the trunk. Red and Sabara are the most common varieties. Rare
Guava
Tropical strawberry guava does well in Perth. Common white guava is very productive in warm spots.
Dragon Fruit
Loves the Perth heat. Comes in red-flesh and white-flesh varieties. Needs something to climb.

WA Quarantine: What Can't Be Shipped In

WA has strict biosecurity laws. Lots of eastern states nurseries can't send plants to WA without a special permit. Check before you order.

  • Bare-root plants: usually can't be sent to WA (soil risk), so WA buyers miss out on most of the eastern states bare-root specials in June-Aug.
  • Citrus: high quarantine risk. A few nurseries have permits, most don't. Check before you order.
  • Stone fruit: similar rules. A few nurseries can ship (Daleys does, seasonally, with a permit), but most can't.
  • Soil-grown root stock: often blocked. Plants grown in pots of potting mix are usually fine.
  • Check the DPIRD website (dpird.wa.gov.au) for the current approved plant list and permit requirements.

Of the 27 nurseries we track, nine can get plants to a WA address. Four ship in from interstate (Daleys Fruit Trees, The Diggers Club, Fruit Salad Trees and Garden Express), and the other five are the WA nurseries above. Shipping policies change, so check the nursery's own site before you order.

WA isn't as cut off as its reputation says. We checked stock once a day for 142 days (March to July 2026), only counting nurseries whose published shipping policy covers each state. 105 of the 108 species we track were available in WA at some point, and on an average day 93.6 of them were in stock somewhere, compared with 101.2 for Victoria. Quarantine costs WA about 7% of the national range, and most of that is a handful of species (Muntries never turned up, Kakadu Plum on 5 days out of 142, Quince on about half).

Tasmania is the state that really misses out. Only three nurseries ship there, 35 of the 108 species were ever available, and 73 were never once buyable in Tasmania over those 142 days.

March-May and September-October are the best times to plant in Perth. December-February is hard on new trees because of the heat (and the fire risk). Bare-root season (June to September) is mostly for deciduous fruit like peaches, plums, apples and pears.

Hard-to-Find Species to Watch For

These don't come into stock often. Set up a stock alert on treestock.com.au so you don't miss them.

  • Jaboticaba: Sabara and Red. Usually only one or two nurseries have it at a time, and it sells quickly.
  • Jujube (rare cultivars): Li, Lang and other named varieties. Chinese sweet dates. Lots of people want them and there aren't many around.
  • White Sapote: Suebelle and Vernon. Grows well in Perth but is rarely stocked in WA.
  • Canistel / Yellow Sapote: turns up now and then at tropical specialist nurseries. Hard to get.
  • Longan: a lychee relative. Sometimes available from interstate nurseries with a WA permit.
  • Cherimoya / Atemoya: African Pride (an atemoya) turns up in WA stock now and then. Custard apple family.
  • Muntries / Quandong: native bush tucker, rare in commercial nurseries.
