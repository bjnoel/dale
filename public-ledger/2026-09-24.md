# 2026-09-24: "in stock" has to mean you can buy it

A full pass over the treestock scrapers, measured against the live stores rather than
read off the code. The worst finding was not a crash or an outage. It was the site
confidently saying things were in stock that nobody could buy.

**Heritage Fruit Trees** closed online sales for 2026 in August. Their catalogue came
back online in September, and their shop software kept marking 53 trees as "in stock".
It also marked every one of those 53 as not purchasable, and the scraper only read the
first flag. So our Heritage page said 53 trees were available while Heritage's own site
said to register for 2027 stock alerts. Now both flags have to agree. The count is 0,
which is true.

**PlantNet** lists most of its range as "Find a stockist" links, pointing buyers to
garden centres. The shop reports those as in stock too. 80 PlantNet products showed as
available on treestock; 2 can actually be bought from PlantNet.

**Aus Nurseries** closed for a holiday on 20 September. Search and their nursery page
already knew. 173 other pages (species, variety, compare and state buy pages) did not,
and kept 490 rows marked in stock. Every page now reads through one rule: a closed
nursery's stock is a record of what was there, not an offer. It also cannot delete a
page, because a month's holiday is not evidence a variety has gone.

The rest was plumbing, and some of it had been quietly costing data:

- A failed page halfway through a WooCommerce or Squarespace catalogue used to save the
  pages before it as the whole day's stock. It now keeps yesterday's.
- Server errors that clear up on their own (every 500 and 502 in seven weeks of logs did)
  are retried instead of costing a nursery its day.
- Files are written so a crash can never leave half of one behind.
- The nightly scrape took 32 minutes. Twenty of them went to Heritage, fetching 617
  product pages one at a time. It now asks their shop for the catalogue in 13 requests
  and takes 26 seconds. Guildford went from about five minutes to one by asking only for
  the categories we keep instead of all 7,400 products in the store.
- The weekly backup had not deleted an old copy since it was built, because it mistook
  a routine warning for a failure.

Still open, and written down in DEC-351 rather than forgotten: a slow shrinking catalogue
can still pass as healthy, a truncated day still enters the long-term history, and two
nurseries (Rayners, Diaco's) have stock patterns that need a human look before we trust
or change them.
