## Two subscribers are receiving email again (DAL-260, closed)

Two of our thirteen treestock subscribers had `plant_categories: []` stored, which made
the digest sender skip them silently. One of them, who signed up on 12 July, had **never
received a single daily digest** in the 22 days since. The code defect was fixed last
week; restoring their delivery was Benedict's call because it is customer-facing.

He said yes. Both are restored to "fruit", which is what the preferences page had been
telling them they were on the whole time, each record stamped with a restore timestamp
and reason so the edit is not silent. Verified rather than assumed: a live dry run of the
sender now names both addresses. They receive from tomorrow's digest.

No backfill and no apology email. Neither subscriber has asked us anything, and a message
explaining that our software had quietly been dropping them is more likely to earn an
unsubscribe than goodwill. Offered to Benedict as his call if he disagrees.

## The nursery register is now something you can open on a phone (DAL-80)

Benedict asked how to see the current status and history of our nursery contacts. That
data has lived in a JSON register readable only from a command line.

It now renders on the existing `/admin` page, which has been behind a Cloudflare Access
gate since June. No new surface, no new authentication, and no public URL for private
relationship notes. Per nursery it shows status, contact route, last touch with days
elapsed, whose move it is next and how long that has been sitting, and a collapsible full
history of every message either way.

It is sorted by what is overdue rather than alphabetically, which makes the state of the
relationship track hard to look away from: **27 nurseries, 7 open actions, 22 never
contacted.** Three nurseries that wrote to us first have been waiting on a reply since
late April. Our single largest referral destination has never been contacted at all.

One column is deliberately missing. Referral click counts are the most useful thing we
could show, and they are read live from Plausible. Putting them on a rendered page would
create a second, staleable copy of the exact number that prices our whole referral plan.
They stay in the tool that fetches them fresh.

## The correction: we have had a working mailbox this entire time

Benedict's other request was a BCC address that Dale can read, so that sending a nursery
email logs itself instead of him doing it from his phone. Before costing out mail
infrastructure, one check:

```
dig MX treestock.com.au
10 in1-smtp.messagingengine.com.
20 in2-smtp.messagingengine.com.
```

That is Fastmail, which Benedict already pays for. **The domain has been receiving email
all along.**

We have had a question on file for weeks stating that replies to our subscriber emails
bounce because we have no address, and it has been blocking a fix and an entire seasonal
email plan. The real defect is narrower and entirely ours: we send from a *subdomain*
that has no MX record, and our mailer sets no `Reply-To` header at all, so replies are
addressed somewhere that cannot receive them. The fix is one line pointing `Reply-To` at
an address on the main domain. Not a new service. Not a DNS change.

The same mailbox answers the BCC request, via an alias and an app password scoped to
reading only. Nothing to buy, nothing to install. We considered routing mail through
Cloudflare instead and rejected it: it would need a DNS change on a live domain, a piece
of code to receive the mail, and a permission we do not hold, all to duplicate a mailbox
that already exists.
