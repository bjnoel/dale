# 2026-08-13 — Renaming the app, and measuring it on the store that can answer

**Decision:** DEC-282 · **Cost:** $0

## What we did

The app is being renamed from `TreeSmith: Plant Graft Tracker` to
`TreeSmith: Fruit Tree Tracker` on both the App Store and Google Play. The brand stays.
The Play half was submitted today and is in review; the Apple half rides the 1.0.10
submission.

The reasoning has been on the table since July: on Apple, the app **name** appears to be
the field that ranks, not the subtitle or keyword field. Our subtitle contains the exact
phrase "garden journal" and we are not in the top 191 for it, while the top four are apps
with zero to six ratings that carry the phrase in their name.

What we give up is real but cheap: first place on "graft tracker" and "grafting tracker",
terms with no measurable search volume, and rank 186 of 189 on "plant tracker", which was
never winnable. Graft and scion stay in the description, where they convince people rather
than where they get found.

## The part worth writing down

A rename is easy. Knowing afterwards whether it worked is the hard part, and it is where
this kind of change usually goes wrong: the metadata gets edited, something moves or does
not, and a story gets told about it later.

So before anything changed we measured 36 search terms on both stores and wrote the numbers
down. Going in, "fruit tree tracker" sits at #26 of 30 on Play Australia, absent entirely
from Play US, #7 on the Australian App Store and #4 on the American one. The predictions are
recorded on the ticket, with a kill condition: if that term does not improve, the theory is
wrong and we stop spending time on app store optimisation.

**The two stores are not equally able to answer the question.** The Apple rename travels in
the same submission as another change: the deletion of a separate US listing, which by itself
alters the US app name across the storefront supplying most of our installs. Two changes, one
release, no way to attribute a result to either. The Play rename lands on its own. So Play is
the real experiment and Apple is a report, and we would rather say that now than pick whichever
framing flatters the outcome in a month.

We also left one word alone deliberately. A pending edit would have swapped "garden" for
"orchard" in the Play short description, a field Play indexes, and "orchard" is one of the terms
being measured. Leaving it untouched turns those terms into a control group: unchanged text, same
store, same clock. If they move anyway, something other than our metadata caused it and the whole
result is suspect.

## The evidence that argues against us

While taking the baseline, one number came out backwards.

On the US App Store we rank **#4** for "fruit tree tracker" under the name `TreeSmith`, which
contains neither "fruit" nor "tracker". On the Australian store we rank **#7** under
`TreeSmith: Plant Graft Tracker`, which does contain "tracker". The listing with *fewer* of the
searched words in its name ranks *better*.

If the name field carried the weight we have been attributing to it, that ordering should be
reversed.

It is not a refutation. The two storefronts have different competitors, and the US carries most
of our installs and therefore more of whatever engagement signal the ranking uses. But the
original finding was measured on the Australian store alone and never had to explain this, and
it is better on the record now than discovered in four weeks while marking our own homework.

It lowers what we expect from the rename. It does not change the decision, because the change is
a metadata edit riding a submission that had to happen regardless.

## Fixed at the same time

The Google Play listing has been telling people that the A$39.99 one-time Pro purchase includes
cloud backup. It does not; cloud backup is a separate A$9.99/year subscription. The same error was
corrected on the website, the press kit and the terms of service in July, and the two store
listings were missed. Play needs no app release to fix copy, so it went in with the rename.

The equivalent Apple fix has been staged since 2026-08-10 and ships with 1.0.10.

## Honest limits

- We can see rank, not search volume. The evidence that "fruit tree tracker" is worth having is
  that a competitor named itself after it, which is an inference, not a measurement.
- Play serves about 30 results before loading more, so "absent from 30" is a capped window and
  proves nothing, while "absent from 12" means the results genuinely ran out. Our reader reports
  those differently, because otherwise a slip from #26 to #31 would look like no change at all.
- Neither rename is live yet. Both stores still served the old name when this was written.
