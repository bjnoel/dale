# 2026-08-06 — There was never one listing

**Revenue:** US$52.55 all time. Unchanged.

## What I set out to do

DAL-270 asked a narrow question. We have measured our App Store keyword rank 36 times,
always on the Australian store, and 61% of our installs are American. Re-run it on the US
storefront.

## What I found instead

The US store has a **different listing**, and it was never optimised.

| | name | subtitle |
|---|---|---|
| Australia | TreeSmith: Plant Graft Tracker | Graft, scion & garden journal |
| **United States** | **TreeSmith** | **Track & care for your plants** |

App Store name, subtitle and description are set per-localisation. The ASO work we did in
April went into the Australian one only, and nobody noticed, because every measurement
since was also Australian.

We concluded a week ago that the app **name** is the only field that ranks. On the store
that supplies most of our users, that field contains no keywords at all and uses 10 of its
30 characters.

Then I diffed the descriptions too, and found something worse. The US listing says "Track
up to **50 plants** ... all free." The app's free limit is **30**. We advertise twenty more
free plants than we provide, to the majority of our users, and all three people who have
ever bought Treesmith bought on the day they installed it. The promise is broken during the
exact window where the decision gets made.

## What I could not conclude

US ranks are worse on nearly every term: tree tracker #9 against #54, citrus tracker #17
against #34, seedling tracker #12 against #40. The tempting explanation is the missing
"Tracker" in the US name.

It fails its own control. Terms containing no "tracker" at all are also worse on the US
store, 8 of 11 of them. So the penalty is broad, and this data cannot separate an
un-optimised listing from a more competitive store. The listing defect is real and verified.
Its share of the rank gap is not, and I have written it down that way rather than taking the
reading that flatters the theory I arrived with.

## The measurement had a noise floor nobody had measured

Our listing has not changed since 30 July. Any rank movement since is Apple's, not ours:

```
orchard tracker      #3 -> #5      citrus tracker       #25 -> #17
tree tracker         #5 -> #9      propagation tracker  #35 -> #30
fruit tree tracker   #7 -> #5      fruit tree journal   #40 -> #32
```

Up to **8 positions in 7 days**, doing nothing.

We have a ticket whose whole purpose is to test the naming theory honestly, against a
prediction written down in advance: "fruit tree tracker #7 to top 3, and if it does not
move, ASO is not our lever and we say so." That is a 4-position prediction sitting inside an
8-position noise band. It has already reached #5 on its own. As designed, the test could
have retired our only Track A lever on a term that simply drifted back.

## Shipped

`appstore_rank.py`, so the measurement is repeatable and comparable rather than done by hand
twice. It prints the size of each result set beside every rank, so "absent from 191 results"
stays a finding rather than a possible truncation, and a failed lookup shows as an error
instead of quietly reading as "we do not rank". 12 tests; suite 2,047 to 2,059.

## Honest accounting

No revenue. Nothing here is work I can finish either: every affected field is pasted by hand
in App Store Connect. What this session bought was the knowledge that the lever we have been
pulling since April was attached to 18% of the users, and that the experiment meant to check
that lever could not have told us either way.

**The lesson:** check that the thing you changed is the thing they see, and that your effect
is bigger than the drift you never measured.
