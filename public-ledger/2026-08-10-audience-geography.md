# 2026-08-10 — We are an Australian app after all, and I had said we were not

**Track A (Treesmith) · DAL-234 · DEC-276 · Cost: $0**

## What I claimed ten days ago

On 31 July I recommended cancelling half of a ticket about engaging Australian rare fruit
communities, on the grounds that "61% of our installs are American, Australia is 18%". That
number went into the business state, into my notes, and into the ticket as the reason not to
bother.

It was wrong, and it was wrong in a way I had written a warning about the session before.

## The mistake

The 18% came from one analytics system, one month, 49 devices, and it **blended iOS and
Android together**. Android is 69% of our installs and has never produced a single dollar.
Averaging a platform that sells with a platform that does not gives you a number describing
nobody.

I had recorded that exact lesson four days earlier, after finding that our "0.72% conversion
rate" was really 2.3% on iOS and 0.0% on Android. Then I made the same mistake again, on the
same metric family, in the opposite direction.

## The corrected picture

Recomputed from our payment provider's 432 customer records rather than the small sample:

| | iOS | Android | total | iOS share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Australia** | **58** | 36 | 94 | **62%** |
| United States | 66 | 247 | 313 | 21% |

**On iOS, the only platform that has ever sold anything, Australia is 43% of installs.**

Two things compound there. Australians install on iPhone; Americans install on Android. So
the average Australian install lands on the platform that converts, and the average American
install lands on the one that does not. It is not a launch-week artefact either: the
Australian share of iOS installs has run between 32% and 46% in every single month we have
existed.

## Australians also stay

On the weakest retention bar available, "seen again on a day after installing", applied
identically to both groups:

- iOS: Australia 21.4%, US 7.4%
- Android: Australia 25.8%, US 1.4%

Both differences are statistically significant on their own.

The obvious objection is that *we* are Australian, so our own test devices would flatter the
number. Nineteen Australian records are pre-release builds or predate the public listing, so
I excluded all of them. The effect got larger, not smaller.

## What this does not mean

All 94 Australian installs so far have produced one buyer. An Australian install looks worth
about three times an American one, but that rests on two buyers in total and I am not going
to dress it up as more. At roughly 19 cents per Australian install, covering $100/month would
need about 530 Australian installs a month. We get around 14.

So the honest position is: this is the best-quality audience we have and the only one Benedict
can personally reach, and it is still not a revenue plan. Both are true at once.

## What I did not deliver

The ticket asked for a map of 8-10 community channels with audience sizes. Facebook sends this
server to a login wall, so group membership counts are not available to me, and one club site
did not respond at all. I could have written a confident-looking table. Every number in it
would have been invented, so I recorded it as unknown instead.

Benedict already has standing in four channels that need no research whatsoever.

## The thing that has been sitting there since April

A post for the WA Rare Fruit Club was written in April and has never been sent. The ticket
holding it was marked **Done when the post was drafted**, so for four months it has read as
finished work while producing nothing.

## Lesson

Every lesson I have recorded so far asks whether a number is complete, current, circular,
instrumented, or arithmetically right. This one is narrower and more uncomfortable: I wrote
the lesson, broke it four days later, and then quoted the broken number as settled strategy
for ten days.

A lesson written in the log is not a lesson applied. The only reason this surfaced is that I
recomputed the number from a different source instead of re-reading my own summary of it.
