# 2026-08-20 — Garden Express did not block us, it changed shops

## What happened

Benedict, this morning: "garden-express seems to have shut down our feed endpoint, is there
another one?"

Since the 2026-08-18 scrape, every request had come back HTTP 400:

```
HTTP 400 fetching https://www.gardenexpress.com.au/wp-json/wc/store/v1/products
                 ?per_page=100&page=1&category=fruit-nut-trees
```

A nursery shutting off its feed is a real thing that happens to us, and it costs goodwill to
get wrong, so the first job was to find out which kind of failure this was. The response
headers answered it:

```
x-redirect-reason: canonical_host_redirection
powered-by: Shopify
server-timing: ... pageType;desc="404"
```

Garden Express moved the entire store from WooCommerce to Shopify, somewhere between our
2026-08-17 scrape and our 2026-08-18 one. Nothing was closed in our direction. The old address
stopped existing because the shop it belonged to stopped existing. Shopify's public product
feed is open, needs no key, and lists 5,617 products.

So: yes, there is another one, and we already had the code to read it.

## The two products that nearly disappeared quietly

The Shopify scraper picks fruit out of a general catalogue by product type. Ten types at
Garden Express are fruit, and they turn out to match the store's own fruit collections almost
exactly, 166 products of 168.

The two that fall through are the ones worth writing down.

**Lemon Meyer has no product type at all.** An empty string, the only real fruit among 5,617
products with nothing in that field. Meyer is one of the most-searched lemons we track. It
would have been dropped with no error raised anywhere, and the site would have said Garden
Express has no Meyer lemon, which is a confident answer and a wrong one.

**Olive Correggiola is filed under "Other Produce Plants",** in with the shallots, the garlic
and the Jerusalem artichokes.

Pulling that thread turned up nine more real fruit stranded in the two mixed produce bins,
including this pair:

```
'Fruiting Vines'  Passionfruit Non Grafted Black     <- picked up
'Edible Produce'  Passionfruit Grafted Black         <- stranded
```

Five of the six passionfruit are typed as vines. The sixth is the grafted black, which is the
one collectors actually ask for. All eleven strays are now listed by name and checked by hand.
The rosemary, rhubarb, wasabi and horseradish sharing those bins are left where they are.

This is the third time an include-filter has silently swallowed real fruit. It never announces
itself; a missing product and a nursery that does not stock it look identical from outside.

## What actually changed

|  | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Products tracked | 103 | 177 |
| In stock | 0 | 29 |
| Berries, currants, blueberries, raspberries | none | 46 |

The berries are not something Shopify gave us. The old configuration listed four tree
categories and the fruiting vines, and had no berry category in it at all. We had been missing
them for as long as we have tracked this nursery.

## What we are not pretending survived

Price and availability history is keyed on the product's web address, and both halves of it
changed:

```
https://www.gardenexpress.com.au/product/lemon-meyer-140mm/    143 days of history
https://gardenexpress.com.au/products/lemon-meyer              starts today
```

The product names differ too, so matching the old records to the new ones would be a guess per
product. We are not making it. The old records stay where they are and stop moving; nothing
reads them as a sell-out. Garden Express price history starts again from today, and the other
fourteen nurseries are untouched.

## The monitoring was already right

Worth saying, because the reflex was to assume something had failed silently for three days.
It had not. An alert went out each night, escalating on its own:

| Night | Alert |
|---|---|
| 2026-08-18 | 0 products today, 103 yesterday |
| 2026-08-19 | HTTP 400 |
| 2026-08-20 | failed three days running |

And no snapshot was saved on any of those nights, so nothing downstream ever saw an empty
shelf. Three dark nights was the system refusing to write down something it did not know,
which is what it was built to do.

## What subscribers see

Tomorrow's comparison runs against the last day we actually observed. Of the 86 varieties
people are watching, five are in the new coverage and exactly one is in stock: a dwarf apple,
one watcher. They will get one email about one tree that really is buyable again. The other
74 newly covered products are sold out and say nothing to anybody.
