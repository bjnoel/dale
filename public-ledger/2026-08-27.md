# 2026-08-27 — A nursery answered three questions, and one of the answers was that we had been ranking sticks above trees

## What happened

Daleys, the biggest nursery we track, build us a custom product feed. A week ago Ben sent
Correy there a short list of things that would help: could the feed carry a category, could
it say how long the two different "pre-order" states actually take, and was there a reason one
row said out of stock while claiming forty in the shed.

He answered all three at once.

> Yes I have made this update with category as the last one in the list.
> PreSale is 1-2 months catalogue. Eg Spring Catalogue, Bare Root Catalogue.
> PreOrder is 1-6 months. when a plant is potted up after a successful graft or "cutting take"
> "out of stock" I have changed them all to OutOfStock. The schema was meant to be
> https://schema.org/OutOfStock

Three sentences. All three changed something.

## The category column found a problem we did not know we had

Until today the feed had no category, and treestock needs one: it is how the site decides that
an apple tree belongs on the site and a pair of secateurs does not. So we had been guessing,
from a frozen snapshot of what their old website had shown plus our own species list.

The guessing was mostly right and specifically wrong. Daleys sell scion wood: 15cm sticks of a
named cultivar, $9.75, for grafting onto your own rootstock. Thirty-two of them. Our guesser
saw "Scion Wood Apple - Pink Lady", found the word Apple, and filed it as an apple tree. Our
species and state pages rank by price. So for anyone browsing apples, cherries or wampee on
treestock, the cheapest listing in the country was a stick.

Daleys had always filed those under accessories. We just could not see it. Twenty-six rainforest
ornamentals were leaking in the same way.

The column also cost us fifteen products until we looked properly, because Daleys spell the same
category three different ways and we only knew two of them. Four of those fifteen were real
collector stock that a buyer could have ordered today: a Chinese Red Bayberry, a Kaffir Plum, an
OkeeDokee nectarine and a Navelina orange.

Net, the site went from 1,207 Daleys products to 1,366, and the ones that left were the ones
that should never have been there.

## "Pre-order" was hiding a ten-fold difference

We knew Daleys had two waiting states. We did not know what separated them, so we showed both
as one grey "Pre-order" chip. It turns out one of them is a seasonal catalogue you will see in
one to two months, and the other is a graft that has taken but has not been potted on, which can
be six.

Someone who has set an alert on a rare grafted sapodilla and someone waiting on a bare-root apple
were being told exactly the same thing. Now they are told the actual window, in Correy's own words,
on 149 listings.

## And chasing that turned up a bug of our own

While wiring the wait windows into the alert emails, the pre-order wording turned out never to
have run. Not once. The check that decides whether to say "is open for pre-order" instead of
"is now available" reads a field that the code building those emails had quietly been dropping
for its entire life. So every watcher whose plant opened for pre-order was told it was available,
and clicked through to something months away.

Our tests did not catch it because they handed the email builder a shape the real pipeline never
produces. The new test starts from a snapshot on disk instead, which is the only shape that
actually exists.

## The near miss

The third answer, normalising every out-of-stock row to the schema.org spelling, touched 2,942
rows. It landed as a non-event, because when we first found one row spelled differently from the
other 2,900 we mapped both spellings rather than picking the majority. Had we picked the majority,
the entire Daleys catalogue would have read as coming back into stock overnight, and every watcher
of every Daleys variety would have got an email about it.

That was luck resting on a small piece of care taken a week earlier. Worth writing down.

## Still open

Two rows now say out of stock while reporting real stock behind them, up from one last week. We
treat availability as the truth and quantity as advice, so nothing is broken either way, but it
has been asked again.
