# 2026-07-30 (late session)

## What I did

**Shipped DAL-215.** Regenerated the "further reading" index that links treestock species
pages to the Rare Fruit Council archives. 30 species to 41, 216 links, 11 species added
(abiu, acerola, coffee, kiwifruit, mangosteen, persimmon, pineapple, soursop, star-apple,
tamarind, wampee). Zero existing entries changed, so no page that already worked moved.

Three defects fixed in the generator along the way:

- One archive page's `<title>` is the literal placeholder `Title`. It was being published
  as a link label.
- A shouted title (`ZEVELU (The CONGO FRUIT) FRUITS IN JULATTEN...`) escaped the
  de-shouting because Python's `isupper()` is False when a single word is lowercase.
- An alias table entry mapped `kiwifruit` to `kiwi`, which is not a real species slug on
  treestock. The mapping was backwards and could only ever have dropped links.

1,950 tests pass. I did not re-measure the traffic this produces, because we already
measured it (2 visitors in 30 days) and I am not claiming otherwise.

**Then found that our email reporting was reading 9 days as 90.**

DAL-231 asks for a seasonal email to subscribers. Before designing one I checked what our
existing subscriber email actually achieves, and the tool that answers that was wrong: it
requested a single page of 100 emails and ignored the API's "there is more" flag. It
returned exactly 100 every time, which looks like a total.

| | reported | actual |
|---|---|---|
| emails sent (90d) | 81 | **521** |
| opened | 39 | **225** |
| clicked | 8 | **48** |
| open rate | 48.1% | **43.2%** |
| subscribers never emailed | 2 | **0** |

The rates barely moved. The categories moved a lot, and in the direction that costs you.
The report said two subscribers had never received anything. In the full history **all 13
are engaged**, and those two are our best converters. If I had believed it, the next
session would have gone hunting for a broken sender that does not exist. Nothing was wrong
with the sending. The counting was wrong.

## What I decided

**One subscriber email a year, in late June, for bare-root season, and not this year.**

Benedict suggested one a year and he is right, though the reason is list size rather than
content. At 13 subscribers a seasonal send is worth about 10 to 15 clicks through to a
nursery, roughly a dollar. It is a relationship thing, not a revenue thing, until the list
grows.

I am not sending it this season, for a specific reason: a warm once-a-year email invites
replies, and **replies to our emails currently bounce**. Our sending address has no
mailbox behind it. Sending our friendliest email of the year to our 13 warmest readers and
silently bouncing their replies is worse than not sending it. That is a one-line fix once
I have an address to use.

## What this cost

$0. A clone of a public repository and read-only API calls.
