# 2026-09-21

## Weekly TreeSmith mail now counts photos and reads store reviews (DEC-346)

Benedict asked for three things in the Monday mail: photos taken, the number of reviews on
each store, and the text of any new review.

Photos turned out to be the app's second most common action and the mail had never
mentioned it: 566 photos added since June against 518 plants, 100 photos in the last week
by 10 people. The app has been sending the event all along; the report just never read it.

The store reviews were the bigger surprise. The project notes said "0 ratings on both
stores". In fact each store holds one five-star written review, one from August on Play and
one from September on the App Store, both from people who found the app themselves. Nobody
had seen either. The mail now lists the count per store and quotes new reviews in full.

Play hides its rating count until an app has enough ratings, so the mail says "not shown by
Play yet" rather than printing a zero it cannot know.

## A subscriber who confirmed, and then received nothing for two weeks (DEC-347)

The delivery alarm named a weekly subscriber who had never received a digest, through two
sends since signing up on the 13th. The first question was whether they had actually
confirmed their address. They had: the confirmation email shows as clicked and the welcome
email as opened.

What happened instead is that 35 seconds after landing on the confirmation success page,
they filled in the preferences picker there, moved themselves from daily to weekly, and
unticked all three "What to include?" boxes. An empty list is the mute-all opt-out, so both
digest senders skipped them from that moment on.

Every sign says the mute was not what they meant. They had just switched themselves to
weekly, which is not what someone does on their way out. "Off" was on the same form, one
section above, labelled "No digest emails, but variety alerts still work", and they did not
choose it. They have no variety watches, so the state they saved left them receiving
nothing from us at all. The likeliest reading is that they took "What to include?" for
opt-in extras on top of a base summary.

This is the same defect as one fixed in August, through the neighbouring field. Both senders
drop a subscriber who has emptied either of two lists. The August fix guarded one of them
and left the other open, and the unguarded one is what caught this person. The API now
refuses both ambiguous states and points at "Off" instead, the picker carries the warning
the settings page has had since August, and the weekly sender now names on stderr anyone it
drops rather than printing a bare count. Its dry run used to list this subscriber under
"Would send to".

Their categories have been restored with Benedict's approval, and he asked that the missed
week not simply be written off, so they have been sent the same digest the rest of the list
received on Sunday. It was delivered. The weekly digest is a single batch on Sunday nights
rather than a rolling week from each signup, so their next one arrives on the 28th with
everyone else's.

Sending it was not quite the end of it. The one-off send path deliberately does not write to
the delivery log, so the alarm would have kept reporting them as never having received
anything, every morning for six days, while the fix sat in place. They were added to
Sunday's log entry by hand. The check now reads "14 active subscribers, all receiving".

Lesson recorded: when a guard is written for one field, check its siblings in the same
condition. The skip tests two lists; August fixed one and the other went on failing for five
weeks.

## The alarm said Dale had halted, and Dale had not (DEC-348)

An email arrived at 12:20 Perth time headed "Dale autonomous run halted", saying the
server's configuration had changed and telling Benedict to delete a STOP file to resume.

Two things were wrong with it. The configuration change was ours: four days earlier we
moved the App Store data pull from weekly to daily, and the Monday recorder that watches
the server for changes nobody decided to make had noticed a change we had decided to make.
That part is the recorder working correctly.

The second thing is that Dale had not halted. It had polled for work at 02:00, 03:00 and
04:00, found none, and carried on doing so after the alarm. There was no STOP file to
delete, and there never has been.

The cause was one email template serving sixteen different failure paths. Two of them are
real stoppages: the breaker that halts Dale after three consecutive failed sessions, and
the strike gate. The other fourteen are a single scheduled job reporting its own trouble
while everything else keeps running. All sixteen used the same headline.

There was already a sign of the problem. One script, the treestock smoke test, had quietly
stopped using the shared alert and written its own email instead, because "autonomous run
halted" was not true of a failed smoke test. It routed around the template rather than
fixing it, and the other fifteen kept the wrong one.

Alerts now come in two shapes. A genuine stoppage keeps the old headline and the old
subject line exactly as it was, because Benedict filters his mail on it. Everything else
says "Dale needs a look" and states plainly that the hourly runs are unaffected. The two
are now distinguishable on a phone without opening either.

The test that guards it walks every place in the codebase that can send one of these
emails and fails if anything outside the runner claims a halt. A job added next year gets
the honest wording by default.

Lesson recorded: an alarm that cannot tell a recording from a stoppage teaches you to
ignore both.
