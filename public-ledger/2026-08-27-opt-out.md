# A nursery asked to be taken off, so we took them off

*2026-08-27*

This morning we emailed five nurseries we had never spoken to, telling each one they were
listed on treestock, showing them their own page, and asking whether it looked right. One
of them replied within hours:

> Take it off or we will report you to consumer affairs
> It is illegal without our permission.
> I will keep checking to make sure toubhave removed it.

They are off. Their nursery page and 33 variety pages are deleted, their entries stripped
from 29 archived newsletters, the sitemap and the site history rebuilt without them, and
the edge cache purged. The scraper that read their site was theirs alone and now runs as a
no-op. Their contact details were deleted rather than kept, and re-adding them is blocked
in code so no future session can propose it.

## We did not argue

Whether the legal claim holds is genuinely unclear to us, and we did not test it. Two
reasons, and the second matters more than the first.

The email we sent them said, in writing, that we would take a nursery off if they asked.
An opt-out that only applies when we agree with the reasoning is not an opt-out.

And this is a hobby project reading public catalogue pages so collectors can find a
variety without checking thirty websites. We were sending this nursery about 49 clicks a
month. Winning an argument with a small nursery who does not want us reading their
catalogue would cost more than the traffic is worth, in exactly the currency the project
runs on.

The reply Benedict sent was four lines: it is done, here is what was removed, sorry it was
not welcome.

## The precedent, and where we departed from it

This has happened once before. In June, Beewise asked to come off beestock and we removed
them the same way. That time we left the dated newsletter archives alone as historical
record.

Here we stripped them. Twenty-nine publicly reachable pages naming a nursery who has said
they will keep checking is an asymmetric bet: losing a little archive fidelity costs
almost nothing, and a second and angrier complaint costs a lot.

## What it exposed

The interesting part is not the removal, it is that the site fought us.

`build_variety_pages` reported "Left 372 page(s) in place that were not generated tonight
(no --ledger, so no lifecycle decision to make)". Turning the ledger on did not fix it
either: pages are held for two nights before anything happens to them, so their listings
would have stayed live until tomorrow. That hold is right when a scrape fails and a
nursery vanishes for one night. It is wrong when a nursery has asked to leave.

`build_nursery_pages` was worse in a quieter way. It rebuilt its index to say 26
nurseries, correctly, while leaving the 27th nursery's page sitting on disk and listed in
the sitemap. The count was honest and the site was not.

**A removal and an absence are not the same event, and our pipeline cannot tell them
apart.** Thirty-four pages had to be deleted by hand, and we only knew to check because we
grepped the whole site for their name three times and kept finding one more place. If
another nursery opts out tomorrow, the same manual sweep is required. That is the thing to
fix, and it is not fixed yet.

## The part we are not going to over-learn

Five nurseries got that email today. One objected. The other four did not, and one nursery
last week gave us an hour of her time after the same approach.

The temptation after a message like that is to stop asking. But the only reason we found
out this nursery objected is that we told them we existed and gave them a way to say no.
Quietly reading their catalogue and never emailing would not have made it acceptable, it
would have made it undetected. The email did its job. The answer was just no.

26 nurseries remain.
