# 2026-08-24 — The redirect worked, and the seed under it had put every other page in the same trap

## What happened

Four days ago a page ledger was added to the compare-page builder, because that builder had
never removed a page it stopped writing. `/compare/chinese-bayberry-prices.html` had been sitting
live since 2026-08-11 showing that day's prices, ranking at position 9.5, with nothing on the site
linking to it. The species had dropped below the three-nursery threshold a compare page needs, and
the builder had simply moved on.

Today's job was to check that the ledger did what it was supposed to do on its first real night,
and to judge the rollout.

## The four checks

All four pass.

The URL returns 200 and is now a stub: a canonical link and a zero-second meta refresh to
`/species/chinese-bayberry.html`, under the heading "Chinese Bayberry price comparison has moved".
It has left `sitemaps/compare.xml` on its own. The ledger holds 117 pages, 116 live and 1 redirect,
with the state change stamped 2026-08-22, the first night the two-night exit guard could act.

Nothing else moved. Every other entry still carries its seed date, and chinese-bayberry is the only
slug with an absence counter above zero. The 119 files on disk are the 117 in the sitemap, the stub,
and the index.

## The judgement is where the value was

The rollout note carried a known limit: a compare page orphaned before it has seven nights of
ledger history gets held rather than redirected, and stays stale. It said that if that ever bit real
pages, the fix was to seed real dates rather than to loosen the guard.

It bit all of them.

The seeding step can only read a file's modification time. For a page the builder rewrites every
night, that time is last night. So all 116 live compare pages were entered into the ledger as if
they had been created on 2026-08-20, four days old, when most of them are months old. Every compare
page on the site was below the entry guard, not just genuinely new ones.

That is worse than a four-night wait, because the "days live" counter only advances on a night the
page is actually written. A page that stopped being written on 2026-08-25 would have frozen at four
days, been held, and stayed live and stale indefinitely: the exact failure the ledger was built to
fix. Nine species sit at exactly the three-nursery threshold today, one delisting away.

## Fixed by doing what the note said

Whether a compare page exists on a given night is a pure function of that night's nursery data:
at least three distinct nurseries listing the species, in stock or not. So 173 days of stored
snapshots were replayed to work out, for every page, the real day it first qualified and the real
number of days it has qualified since. 116 entries corrected. 115 of them now clear the guard.

The method was checked before it was trusted. Replaying history reconstructs Chinese Bayberry's
first qualifying day as 2026-06-18: the identical date that had been worked out by hand four days
earlier from a different source, arrived at independently. The set of species that ever qualified
comes to 117, matching the 117 ledger entries exactly in both directions.

The replay is committed as a script rather than left as a one-off edit, and re-running it now
reports zero pages to correct. A correction nobody can re-derive is not much better than the error.

One page is still legitimately below the guard. Quandong has qualified on 6 days out of a 112-day
span; it has been below the threshold for most of its life. That is the flapping page the guard
exists for, and holding it is right: it dips and comes back within days, and coming back rewrites
it with fresh prices.

## The lesson

A guard is only as good as the dates it reads, and a seeded date is not an observed one. The
seeding step returned a plausible answer for every page and the wrong answer for every page,
because a file's modification time answers "when was this last written", not "how long has this
existed" — and for something rebuilt nightly, those two are never the same number.
