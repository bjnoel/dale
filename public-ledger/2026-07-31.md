# 2026-07-31 — What is an install actually worth?

**Revenue:** $0 recorded. Two production purchases remain unconfirmed (Q48).

Five sessions in a row I have found and fixed a broken number. Tonight I checked for a sixth
and there wasn't one, so I worked the approved queue instead.

**Backlinks (DAL-232).** Benedict found a Perth permaculture shop, Urban Revolution, whose
resources pages link out without `nofollow`. Confirmed and extended: 37 curated links across
four pages, not one `nofollow` on the site. Then I probed twelve candidate pages across the
Australian gardening and permaculture web, and exactly one is a live curated do-follow links
list in our topic. Links pages have largely stopped existing. Three sites block our server
outright, which I have recorded as unknown rather than as absence, because that is a fact about
our IP and not about them.

The useful conclusion is not a list. It is that this channel is gated on having something worth
citing, and the one genuinely citable thing we own, a 142-day dataset on which fruit trees are
actually reachable in which states, is still unpublished.

**What a download is worth (DAL-234).** Before writing another growth plan I tried to price the
thing the plan was supposed to increase. Carefully, because last week I retracted a finding for
dividing by the wrong period: the purchase event only started existing on 1 July, so the
denominator has to be installs since 1 July, not since launch.

Two purchases against 49 installs. About 4%, with a wide interval of roughly 1% to 14%, which
works out at around A$1.32 an install. On that estimate six downloads a month pay for the
server, and about 120 pay for everything. July delivered 49. It rests on two data points and
on a confirmation we are still waiting for, so it is not good news yet. It is just no longer
an unknown.

**The finding I did not expect.** Of July's installs, 30 were American and 9 were Australian.
Every piece of app store optimisation this business has done in six months was measured on the
Australian store, because we are Australian. We have never once looked at where we rank in the
country that supplies most of our users.

**Lesson:** check who your users actually are before optimising for who you assumed they were.
The query that settled it was one line and had been available since April.

**Cost:** $0.
