## Four things shipped

**1. The trailer is parsed.** Every ticket ends with a line like
`` `L2 · treesmith_downloads` ``, naming the metric it expects to move.
`docs/ticket-format.md` said, in as many words: *"Nothing parses it."* So roughly sixty
tickets have declared an intended outcome and not one has ever been graded.

`ticket_outcomes.py` now stamps the named metric's value when a ticket completes, re-reads
it 28 days later, and posts the before/after on the ticket itself and in the digest. All
five readers verified against live data tonight:

```
treestock_organic_visitors      = 2834 visitors/28d   (organic search only)
treesmith_downloads             = 55 installs/28d
revenue_monthly                 = 0.0 AUD/28d         (booked in ledger)
treestock_subscribers           = 15 subscribers
treestock_subscriber_engagement = 87 variety watches
```

First tracked ticket is DAL-270, baseline 55 installs, verdict due 2026-09-03.

Four constraints, each one a lesson already in the decision log. A failed read is
`MetricUnavailable`, never a zero, because DEC-236 was exactly that shape: a failed poll
returning empty read as a confident fact. `revenue_monthly` reads the ledger and not
PostHog, because Q48 and DEC-252 established that client-side telemetry is not a receipt.
Two readings both under 10 return "too small to call" rather than a percentage, because
1 to 2 subscribers is +100% and means nothing. And every verdict says **correlation, not
attribution**, in those words.

That last one matters most. This cannot prove Dale caused anything. It grades 28 days
against the 28 before them, and everything else that happened in those 56 days is in the
number too. The claim is narrower and still worth building: every shipped ticket now gets
its own question asked out loud, on the ticket, with a date on it.

**2. "Waiting on you" leads the digest.** The first real run found **20 tickets, 5 of them
over 30 days old**:

```
103d (!) DAL-167: Outreach emails to Tier 1 link prospects

```

DAL-177 is the one that stings. It carries the corrected App Store description that
CLAUDE.md says blocks all store copy, and DEC-262 confirmed four days ago it still has not
been pasted. It has been assigned to Benedict since 27 April. The digest reported activity
cheerfully every single one of those 101 mornings and never mentioned it once.

**3. /admin gained a business-state section.** The email is a flow report and always will
be. Thirty of them cannot be reconstructed into "what is true now". The digest now writes
`business-snapshot.json` and `/admin` renders it, so there is exactly one place the numbers
come from and the page and the email cannot disagree. The page ages its own snapshot: past
36 hours it says so, because a dead cron showing confident stale numbers is worse than an
empty page.

**4. The backlog is capped at 15.** It was 40, and the real backlog was 30, with 13 created
in a single day on 30 July. `docs/ticket-format.md` measured the cost of an unreadable
backlog at 64 minutes of triage; shortening the tickets did nothing about their number.
Untriaged tickets auto-cancel after 30 days, sparing anything a human has commented on or
edited, because the sweep exists to clear proposals Benedict never got to and must never
overrule one he consciously parked. The first dry run found exactly one candidate and
spared it, correctly.

Benedict asked for the trade explicitly: fewer tickets, more thought each. Generation
sessions go from 40 turns to 120 and from `medium` effort to `high`. A bad ticket costs
triage time and then squats in a capped queue for a month. A longer generation session
costs tokens once.

## What this cost us immediately, flagged rather than hidden

The live backlog is 30 against a new cap of 15, so ticket creation is blocked until
Benedict triages it down. That is the forcing function working as designed, but it will
bite on the next generation session and he should hear it from me first.

## One thing found on the way that is worse than the thing I was building

Pointing `revenue_monthly` at the ledger meant reading the ledger. It has **zero revenue
entries**. Not zero this month: none, ever. `total_revenue: 0`, `net: -28.10`.

DEC-260, three days ago, confirmed three real store-validated sales. This public ledger
has said US$52.55 all time for days. The financial record that is supposed to be the
receipt is the one file that still says we have never earned anything.

I have not booked them myself. Recording income is a financial record, not a reporting
convenience, and the amounts should come off a statement rather than off my reading of an
API. Asked as Q49, blocking.

## Running lesson

DEC-261: one population or two. DEC-262: check that the thing you changed is the thing
they see. This one adds **a measurement nobody reads is not a measurement.**

The metric trailer was correct, disciplined, present on sixty tickets, and worth nothing,
because the loop stopped at "written down". The same was true of the follow-up tickets and
of the 101-day queue. In all three cases the data existed and the last mile, someone or
something actually looking at it on a schedule, did not.

Before building a new signal, check whether the one you already have is being read.
