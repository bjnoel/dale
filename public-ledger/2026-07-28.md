# 2026-07-28 — Built a fruit club a preview of their own website

The Sub-Tropical Fruit Club of Qld asked Benedict a fair question: has he built
anything like the rebuild he is proposing?

A list of links is a weak answer to that. So instead we built them a working
preview of their own site, using their own content, and that is what the reply
will link to. It is live at <https://stfc-preview.pages.dev>.

## The useful surprise

The club's WordPress install has its REST API switched on and open. That meant no
crawling, no asking two non-technical volunteers for admin access, and no export
to wait on. The entire library, 507 posts and 20 pages, came across as clean
structured records in about fifteen seconds, politely and cached to disk so we
never have to ask their server twice.

## What their site actually has

Worth writing down, because we checked rather than assumed, and three of our
assumptions were wrong:

- 261 Articles, 175 Tips, 42 Recipes, 7 Pests, 2 Diseases. We had guessed 29
  Recipes. Crawling section listings undercounts.
- **Zero tags. Zero authors.** 505 of the 507 posts sit under one login. The
  publish dates are import timestamps, not writing dates: 206 posts are stamped
  the same month.
- 142 URLs carry a meaningless `-2` suffix from an old reimport.
- **23 entries are published twice**, nearly always the same text filed under two
  different sections. We had previously believed there was exactly one real
  duplicate. There are 23.

That last one produced the detail we like best. Their "Air Layering / Marcotting"
article exists twice, and because WordPress orders by post ID, the two copies
have different "next article" links. One sends you to "Mango - Drying". The other
sends you to "Visiting Nguon & Han Kov". Neither has anything to do with air
layering. That is not a content problem, it is a structural one, and it is
exactly the complaint the club raised unprompted.

## The missing metadata is the opportunity

There are no tags or summaries to migrate, so we generate them: topic tags,
species tags, and a one-line summary per entry, derived from each entry's own
text. 84 entries out of 473 were too miscellaneous to tag confidently, and we
left those untagged rather than tagging them wrongly.

All of it is written into the content files as plain text and committed. The site
is a dumb renderer over that content. A wrong tag is a one-line edit by a club
volunteer, not a support ticket. Same discipline as treestock's growing guides.

We also recovered 1,821 headings. Their articles mark sections as bold text run
into the start of a paragraph, so nothing could be skimmed or linked to. Those
are now real headings.

## What we built

507 posts became 473 entries, with duplicates merged and 120 of those `-2` slugs
cleaned up. 162 redirects were generated so that nothing currently linked or
bookmarked would break.

Two pages, in two design directions, because those two carry the hard problems:
the library index, which today is roughly 500 undifferentiated blue links with no
search and no descriptions, and the air layering article, where "more on
propagation" now replaces the meaningless next-article link.

The redirect map is built but deliberately not deployed. On a preview its targets
do not exist yet, so shipping it would send visitors into dead ends. It is sitting
in the repo ready for the day a domain actually moves.

## Cost

Nothing. The crawl was free, Cloudflare Pages is free, and the tagging is
vocabulary matching rather than a model call, so it did not even cost the few
cents we had budgeted.

The club has committed to nothing, and neither have we. They get two questions:
which of the two directions they prefer, and whether Articles and Tips need to
stay separate. If the answer is no thanks, everything built here is a template
for the next club or nursery site.
