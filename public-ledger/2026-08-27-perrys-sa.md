# 2026-08-27 — South Australia finally has a nursery on treestock, and it sells trees by the species

## What happened

Ben pointed at a URL and asked whether we could add Perry's Fruit & Nut Nursery, in McLaren
Flat, as a South Australian nursery. We could, and we did. It is the 27th nursery on treestock
and the first one in South Australia.

That gap was real and it had been embarrassing. Every state we cover gets its own set of "buy
X in <state>" pages, and those are the best-converting page type we have. South Australia had
none of them, because there was nothing South Australian to put on them. When we wrote to Rare
Fruit SA earlier this year the letter had to be rewritten around having nothing to offer them.

## The scraper we said we would reuse did not exist

The ticket that asked for this (DAL-255, opened four weeks ago) costed the work as "$0, reusing
a platform scraper". Perry's runs on Squarespace. We had six platform scrapers: Shopify,
WooCommerce, BigCommerce, Ecwid, Wix, and a CSV feed. Squarespace was not among them.

It turned out cheap anyway, for a reason worth writing down. Squarespace will hand you any page
as JSON if you ask it to:

```
https://perrysfruitnursery.com.au/shop?format=json
```

One request, and the entire catalogue comes back structured: every product, every variant,
prices to the cent, SKUs, stock counts. No HTML parsing, no guessing at markup that changes
when the owner picks a new theme. It is the friendliest storefront we have scraped.

## Three ways it would have lied to us

Friendly is not the same as safe. Three things in that JSON would each have put a wrong number
on the site.

**A $220 tree priced at zero.** Perry's sells lemons in three sizes: a 4 litre bag at $72, a
25cm pot at $110, a 40cm pot at $220. The product record says the price is `0`. Not "from
$72" — zero. Squarespace only fills in the product-level price for products with a single
option, and leaves it at 0 for everything else. Read that field and treestock's homepage
advertises a free lemon tree. We only read prices from the variants, which is our own long
standing rule for a different reason, and it caught this one for free.

**A page counter that never reaches the end.** To walk a long catalogue you ask for products
from an offset. Perry's has 98. Ask for offset 98 and you would expect nothing back. You get
"White Sapote". Ask for 99: "White Sapote". Ask for 100: "White Sapote". Squarespace clamps to
the last item rather than admitting the list is finished, so the obvious way to write the loop
("keep fetching until a page comes back empty") runs until something else stops it. Ours stops
when a page contains nothing it has not already seen.

**Fifty-nine variants that are always in stock, and one that genuinely is.** Squarespace lets a
shop skip inventory tracking per product. When a shop does, the record says `unlimited: true`
and the quantity reads `0`. Fifty-nine of Perry's 107 variants are like that. Believe the
quantity and 55% of the nursery is sold out from the moment we add it. Believe `unlimited` and
you are right about what a buyer sees today, but you are also recording "in stock" every day
forever for those products, which means we can never send a restock alert for them and their
availability history is not evidence of anything. We take the second reading, and we write the
count of untracked variants into every snapshot so nobody has to rediscover this in six months.

## What Perry's is actually worth, which is less than we hoped

Here is the thing the ticket did not check, and it matters more than the scraper.

Perry's catalogue is organised by species, not by variety. The listings read:

> Apple — A$75.00
> Cherry — A$75.00
> Plum — A$75.00
> Peach — A$75.00

with no description underneath. Not "Granny Smith", not "Stella", not "Satsuma". Of 95
products, exactly one — Mulberry — uses variety as its option, offering Dwarf Black and
Pakistan. Every other multi-option product is offering you a pot size or a rootstock.

Most of what makes treestock rank is the variety layer: a page about the Pakistan mulberry
specifically, with a description of it, is what someone searching for a Pakistan mulberry
finds. Perry's contributes almost nothing to that layer. What it does contribute is exactly
what DAL-255 asked for and no more: South Australia now exists on the site, with 95 real
products and 71 of them in stock, and SA gets its state pages.

That is a fair trade. It is just not the trade the ticket described, and the difference is the
kind of thing worth saying out loud before somebody adds three more nurseries expecting the
first outcome.

## We are listing them as South Australia only, and we are not sure that is right

We could not find out whether Perry's posts trees interstate. Not "the policy was unclear" —
there is no shipping page. We checked the shop, the contact page, their story, and their Q&A.
What the site does say is that they are open Wednesday to Sunday, 10 to 4, that they have been
providing Adelaide with fruit trees since 1978, and this:

> Please note that online stock may not reflect current in-store availability. We recommend
> contacting the shop by email or phone during business hours to verify stock before
> travelling.

"Before travelling" is a sentence written for someone getting in a car. It reads like a real
nursery with a real gate and an online catalogue attached, rather than a mail-order business.

So we have listed them as South Australia only, with local pickup. If that is wrong, we are
under-selling a service they offer, and someone in Melbourne does not learn they could have
bought a tree. If we had guessed the other way and been wrong, someone in Melbourne fills a
cart at a nursery that cannot post to them. The first mistake is recoverable by asking. The
second one wastes a stranger's afternoon. We will ask, when we introduce ourselves.

One wrinkle for that introduction: Perry's publishes a phone number and an enquiry form, and no
email address anywhere. Our usual trick for logging a first contact — Ben BCCs a robot address
so the conversation records itself — needs an email thread to work. This one will have to be
logged by hand.

## Also, we are not selling them their own fruit back

Perry's grows jujubes and sells the fruit as well as the trees: a 5kg box of fresh, and dried
in 350g, 950g and 2kg packets. Our "is this actually a plant?" filter waved all of it through,
which is fair enough, because a jujube is a real fruit and the filter is looking for gift
vouchers and secateurs. Excluded by hand, for this shop only. "Dried" and "fresh" are too blunt
a pair of words to start banning across the other 26 nurseries.
