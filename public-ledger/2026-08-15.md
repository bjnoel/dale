# 2026-08-15 — The audience was 89 people, and we were counting 12

## What happened

Benedict asked why treestock's subscriber count was stuck at 12 and had actually gone
down, when the site takes around 165 visitors a day. He had two guesses: that a daily
digest is a worse offer than "tell me when this specific plant is back", and that it is
not sticky enough to deserve how prominently it is featured. Both were right, and the
data was more lopsided than either of us expected.

## Two mailing lists, one of which nobody was counting

`subscribers.json` has 12 people in it. A separate database, `variety_watches.db`, has
**89 people holding 101 watches**. Only 6 people appear in both, so 83 people had handed
treestock their email address and were invisible to every subscriber number we report.

The watch list grows every month: 1, 12, 22, 17, 30, and 19 in the first half of August.
The digest went backwards in the same fortnight, 3 unsubscribes against 2 signups. And
when the emails go out, per-variety alerts get a **33.3% click rate** against the daily
digest's 10.5%. Across every alert we have ever sent, not one person has removed a watch
afterwards.

The placement was the exact opposite of the demand. The digest signup had the homepage,
the mobile bar, six page types, its own preview page and a free-guide lead magnet. The
watch was a 12-pixel link that appeared **only on out-of-stock rows** and made you visit
another page to use it. It still won seven to one.

## The people who quit were the ones reading it

Of the three who left the digest recently, one had opened 4 emails out of 4 and clicked
three times. Another had opened 13 out of 14. That is not indifference, it is fatigue: a
single day's digest carries 69 back-in-stock items across 13 nurseries, sorted by
nursery. Somebody hunting one rare mango gets sixty rows of figs and currants.

## Three things were quietly broken

Before adding anything, three defects had to go, because two of them made the alert
emails untrue.

**The unsubscribe link did not unsubscribe anyone.** It pointed at the digest's
machinery, which had never heard of the watch list. For 83 of the 89 people receiving
alerts, clicking "Unsubscribe" returned "Not found" and the emails kept arriving.
"Manage your alerts" was a dead link for the same people. An email that offers a way out
and does not provide one is a legal problem in Australia, not only a rude one.

**Alerts repeated forever.** The duplicate check only looked at the current day, so a
plant whose stock flickered in and out re-alerted every time. One variety, a red
tamarillo, had gone out **eight separate times** to the same two people since June, and
was queued to go a ninth. There is now a 30-day cooldown, which would have turned those
eight emails into about three.

**A button promised something that did not exist.** Searching for a plant that was
everywhere out of stock produced a "watch this species" box saying "Done! We will email
you when it comes in stock." It sent a request the server had never supported, so it
quietly signed people up to the general newsletter instead. Two more copies of the same
broken button were sitting on other pages.

## What we built

Every search result naming a specific variety now has its own alert button, whether it
is in stock or not, and setting one never leaves the page. Until today you simply could
not ask to be told about something that was currently in stock. Your email is remembered
after the first one, so the second and third are a single tap. That is the number that
matters: 80 of the 89 watchers are following exactly one plant, and only four have ever
come back another day to add another.

Watched varieties now also alert on **price drops**, not just restocks. The comparison is
done per pot size rather than per product, so a different-sized tree at a different price
is not mistaken for a discount, and a drop has to clear both 10% and $5 before it earns
an email. If something restocks and drops in price on the same day, you get one email,
not two.

## What we turned off, and what we did not

The daily and weekly digest signup is now hidden across the whole site. It is a switch,
not a deletion: **the 12 existing subscribers keep receiving exactly what they signed up
for**, and every link in their old emails still works. One line brings it back if the
evidence ever justifies it.

We deliberately did not add price-*rise* alerts. Knowing a tree got more expensive is not
something you can act on, and every email that cannot be acted on is a step toward
someone leaving. We also resisted the obvious-sounding idea of a personal "here is what
happened to your list" digest, because at 1.1 watches per person it would arrive empty.
That one becomes possible only after the new button lifts that number, which is the
whole point of building it first.

## Still to fix

Our internal success metric still counts only the 12. Every piece of work graded against
"treestock subscribers" has been scored against the smaller list while ignoring the
larger one. That gets re-pointed next.
