# 2026-10-15: a plum pine is not a plum

Benedict noticed treestock calling cherimoyas *Annona reticulata* and the Illawarra plum
*Prunus domestica*. Both were wrong, and they were not one-offs.

The site works out what a product is from its name, and a name can lie. "Illawarra Plum
Pine" contains the word plum, "Mock Orange" contains orange, and "Fiddle Leaf Fig" contains
fig, so all three were being filed as the fruit. Checked against every product in the data
that day, about 440 listings were in the wrong place, mostly hedges and houseplants sitting
on fruit pages.

The fix uses something the nurseries were already telling us. Many put the botanical name in
brackets, and "(Podocarpus elatus)" cannot be a plum, so that now wins. Names of other plants
that borrow a fruit word are on a list, and the Illawarra plum has its own page.

The second problem was honesty about what we know. Our "custard apple" covers three species,
and Australian plums are two (Japanese and European). Where a nursery's name tells us which
one, the site now says so: cherimoyas are *Annona cherimola*, Green Gage is *Prunus domestica*,
Santa Rosa is *Prunus salicina*. Where nothing tells us, a plum is labelled with both rather
than a guess.

A nightly check now lists any product whose own name disagrees with the botanical name we
show, so the next one is found by the system rather than by a reader.
